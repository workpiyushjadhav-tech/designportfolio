Get to know me

A few things about how I think, work, and what makes me... me.

COVID interrupted my computer engineering degree, and I pivoted into design out of curiosity more than a plan. The first couple of years were self-taught and full of rejections — but they stuck.

At Zeus Learning, I built WCAG-compliant ed-tech interfaces for clients like McGraw Hill ALEKS. That work taught me accessibility isn't a checklist you run at the end — it's a design decision you make from the first sketch.

I had three and a half years of shipped work but no formal grounding in the research behind it. I came to the University of Lancashire to complete that picture, not start over.

On the DWP Bereavement Support Payment redesign. Designing a government service for people going through one of the worst weeks of their life makes clarity and accessibility non-negotiable, fast.

Somewhere between visual craft and UX research — I sketch and ship fast, but I've come to trust the slower, structured methods just as much as instinct.

Service design with real stakes — civic and public-sector work like GOV.UK services, where good design isn't decoration, it's the difference between someone getting help or giving up.
